# Gonka Capital — DECISIONS

Judgment calls, and why. Newest first.

## 2026-09-22 — Consensus is a direction, not an action

A purchase of put options is a bet a stock falls. Counting it as "buying" because
the filing says "purchase" would be flatly wrong, and there are 14 such rows in
the current data. Every row therefore resolves to bullish or bearish, and the few
genuinely two-sided cases (selling a put, closing a put position) are excluded
rather than guessed at. Put and call are stated in every options filing in the
corpus; a check found zero rows where it could not be read.

## 2026-09-22 — Anyone pointing both ways is dropped from that company

Gottheimer rolls Microsoft options: on 4 of his 14 Microsoft days he bought and
sold the same position. A naive count made him one of "6 people selling
Microsoft" when he had no directional view at all. Excluding any person who moved
both ways on a company inside the window removes 19 person-company pairs out of
several hundred, which is a cheap price for not publishing a false signal. It
also subsumes the narrower same-day-roll rule, so there is only one rule to
explain.

## 2026-09-22 — Align on when a trade happened, not when it was filed

A 13F filed in August describes April to June. A congressional trade is dated to
the day it happened. Aligning on filing dates would put an investor's April move
and a politician's September move in the same "window" while describing periods
that never overlap. Investor moves are therefore dated to their quarter end.

## 2026-09-22 — Consensus replaced the planned composite portfolio

The plan had two features: a consensus view and a "what would I own" list. Of the
228 tickers the investors hold, only 26 are held by more than one, so the second
list either runs to 228 names or collapses into the first. One feature, not two.

## 2026-09-22 — Agreed is the first tab and the opening screen

The app opened on a reverse-chronological ledger that was half two people. The
screen that answers the reader's actual question should be the one he lands on.
No habit was broken: the app had not been delivered yet.

## 2026-09-22 — 180 days, minimum two people

180 days spans two 13F quarters and six months of congressional trades, which
keeps both halves of the app represented. At 90 days the investor side is a
single quarter and the list thins. One person is not a consensus.

## 2026-09-22 — The honesty rules are printed where the numbers are

The exclusion rule and the put rule are stated at the bottom of every company
sheet, not buried in About. A reader who wonders why a name is missing finds the
answer next to the count.

## 2026-09-22 — No price data, and no performance

Deliberately not built. Range-based disclosures published weeks late cannot
produce an honest return figure, and a chart implying otherwise would be the most
misleading thing the app could show.
