# Who Gonka Capital follows, and why

Two rules decided this list.

1. **The trades have to be verifiable.** Every name here files a legally required,
   public report that says what they bought or sold. No newsletters, no stock
   pickers, no "guru" whose portfolio is a guess.
2. **Following has to be possible.** A fund holding six hundred positions and
   turning them over weekly is not something a person can follow, so those are
   left out no matter how good the returns.

## Investors

They file **Form 13F** with the SEC: a list of US-listed stock holdings, due 45
days after the end of each quarter, required of every manager holding more than
$100 million in US equities. Gonka Capital reads the filing itself and compares each
quarter with the one before it.

| Who | Firm | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Warren Buffett | Berkshire Hathaway | The longest public track record in investing. |
| Bill Ackman | Pershing Square | About a dozen concentrated positions, held for years. |
| Li Lu | Himalaya Capital | Charlie Munger's pick to manage family money. Rarely trades. |
| Mohnish Pabrai | Dalal Street | Concentrated value portfolio, openly modeled on Buffett. |
| Stanley Druckenmiller | Duquesne Family Office | One of the best long-run macro records. Moves faster than the others. |

| Chris Hohn | TCI Fund Management | A dozen high-quality compounders held for the long run. |
| Terry Smith | Fundsmith | Buy good companies, don't overpay, do nothing. |
| Gates Foundation Trust | Gates Foundation Trust | A few very large, very patient positions. |

### Considered and left out

- **Michael Burry (Scion).** Stopped filing after the third quarter of 2025. There
  is nothing current to show, so he is not on the list. The pipeline checks this
  every run and warns if a followed manager goes quiet.
- **David Einhorn (Greenlight Capital Inc).** That entity last filed in 2024.
- **Bridgewater, Renaissance, Citadel, Millennium.** Hundreds to thousands of
  positions, rebalanced constantly. Nothing a person can act on.
- **Any fund whose main strategy is shorting, credit or derivatives.** A 13F shows
  long US equity positions only, so the filing would paint a false picture.

### One structural note

Pershing Square's holdings moved in 2026 from Pershing Square Capital Management
to its public parent, Pershing Square Inc. Gonka Capital follows both filer entities
so the portfolio does not appear to vanish. Firms restructure; the config allows
several filer IDs per person for exactly this reason.

## Politicians

Members of Congress must report every trade within 45 days under the **STOCK Act**
of 2012. The House publishes reports through the Clerk; the Senate publishes them
through its Office of Public Records. Two things limit what any app can show:

- Amounts are **ranges** ($1,001 to $15,000, and so on), never exact figures.
- Some members file **on paper**, as scanned images. Gonka Capital does not guess at
  those. A member's page says how many of their reports could not be read.

The list is the members whose 2026 filings are electronic, frequent enough to be
worth watching, and large enough to be meaningful. It was chosen by counting
actual parsed trades, not by reputation.

| John Hickenlooper | CO | Infrequent but large trades. |

### Considered and left out

- **Ro Khanna, Michael McCaul, Diana Harshbarger, Hal Rogers.** High filing
  volume, but they file on paper. Nothing machine-readable to show.
- **Members whose trades are all municipal bonds or treasuries.** Not stock
  trades, so there is nothing to follow.
- **Members who have left office.** The pipeline checks every followed member
  against the official House and Senate membership lists on each run and marks
  anyone who is no longer serving.

## What this list is not

It is not a claim that these people beat the market, and Gonka Capital shows no
performance numbers, because none can be calculated honestly from range-based,
45-day-late disclosures. It is a list of people whose decisions are a matter of
public record and worth reading.
